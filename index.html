Human–AI Slider Model

Productive Struggle ↔ Cognitive Delegation

HUMANProductive Struggle AICognitive Delegation
    About this framework

    A thinking tool, not rules. It helps you decide how to collaborate with AI based on your learning goals and your competence in the task at hand. You keep control of the goals and the decisions; the framework only makes the trade-off visible.

    The spectrum. Each task sits somewhere between two poles. On the left, Productive Struggle — you do the thinking yourself, reaching for AI only after genuine effort. On the right, Cognitive Delegation — you intentionally divide the thinking work (not just the mechanical work) with AI, while keeping control of the goals. Most real work lands in between, and a task can move along the slider as you go.

    Understanding is non-delegable. AI can do many things for you. It cannot understand for you. Sliding toward AI delegates the task, not the learning — a skill is yours when you can do it without AI. Worth keeping in view whenever the point is for someone to learn, not just to finish.

    Two ways to use it. Diagnostically — “which approach am I currently using, and does it match my goal?” Prescriptively — “given my goal and constraints, which approach should I adopt?” Educators tend to use it prescriptively (scoping an assignment); learners diagnostically (reflecting on what they actually did).

    One axis, honestly. This is a single Human↔AI spectrum. It deliberately doesn’t capture whether you’re learning a skill or applying one already mastered — the same setting can mean opposite things in those two cases. Read each one with that question in mind, and give every task its own slider rather than collapsing a whole assignment to one.

    On shared links. A read-only link records a shared intent, not a lock. It says where the work is meant to sit; it can’t police where the work actually happens. Treat it as a conversation starter — anyone can open an editable copy.

    Prerequisites. This model assumes the ethical, legal, equity, environmental, and academic-integrity questions have already been considered and appropriate tools selected.
